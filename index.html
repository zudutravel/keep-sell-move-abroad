Rules this model is built on

General information as at October 2026, not tax or financial advice. Get a registered tax agent to confirm your residency and any sale before you sign.

Interest as a foreign resident

  • Once your bank records you as a non-resident with an overseas address, it withholds a flat 10% of interest. That is a final tax: no return needed for it, no deductions against it.
  • As an Australian resident, interest is added to your taxable income and taxed at 15%, 30%, 37% or 45% plus the 2% Medicare levy (2026–27 rates, $18,200 tax-free).
  • Tell each bank about your status change. If it still thinks you're a resident, it won't withhold correctly and you'll owe returns.

Selling the house

  • If you are a foreign resident on the day you sign the sale contract, you lose the main residence exemption entirely, even for the years you lived there. The 50% CGT discount is also lost for the time you were non-resident.
  • Signing while still an Australian resident keeps the exemption. That's the "Exempt" option on the Sell tab.
  • Without an ATO clearance certificate, the buyer withholds 15% of the price at settlement. Residents can get one; foreign residents claim the excess back through a return.
  • The six-year absence rule lets a resident rent the old home and still claim it as their main residence. It does not help if you're a foreign resident when you sell.
  • A narrow life-events test (six years or less away, plus a specific event such as a terminal illness, death or divorce) can restore the exemption for foreign residents.

Renting it out from overseas

  • Foreign residents pay 30% from the first dollar of net rent (no tax-free threshold), 37% above $135,000 and 45% above $190,000. No Medicare levy.
  • Losses from a negatively geared property carry forward and offset future rental profit. This model carries them forward year by year.

Bank-hopping

  • Most top rates are 3 to 6 month intro offers for customers new to that bank. Track the banking group, not just the brand: Ubank sits under NAB; Bankwest under CBA; St.George, Bank of Melbourne and BankSA under Westpac.
  • Check balance caps. Rates often apply only up to $50k, $100k, $250k or $500k, with a lower rate above.
  • Many bonus accounts need monthly balance growth, deposits or no withdrawals. If you live off the interest, draw it from an account without that rule.

Living in Vietnam

  • Spending 183 days or more in Vietnam in a year can make you a Vietnamese tax resident, which in principle brings worldwide income into scope. The Australia–Vietnam tax treaty limits double tax on interest. Worth confirming with a Vietnamese adviser.
